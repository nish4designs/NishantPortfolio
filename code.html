Aera Discovery · New Approach

From “build the analysis” to
“discover the answer”

The new approach changes the starting point of Discovery: instead of requiring the user to construct the analytical path, the conversation becomes the entry point and Discovery helps determine what needs to be explored.

Core problem: Users often know the business problem, but they don't necessarily know which data, dimensions, filters or visualizations will reveal the answer.

Historical Context

Earlier Discovery: From Data Exploration to Business Understanding

Earlier Discovery could visualize and explore data effectively, but it had a fundamental limitation: it had limited understanding of the user’s intent and the business context behind the data.

The user still had to translate a business question into the right analytical approach.

The typical flow
Business question → User selects data → Configures analysis → Visualizes results → Interprets findings
ℹ

In other words, Discovery supported the analysis, but the user was responsible for much of the analytical reasoning.

The limitation was therefore not primarily about the quality of the charts or visualizations. The deeper gap was between:

Starting intent

“What business question am I trying to answer?”

Analytical hurdle

“What analysis should I perform to answer it?”

Example Scenario

“Why are inventory levels increasing in North America?”

To answer this effectively, the system needs to reason through several things:

1 What does “inventory increasing” mean in this business context?
2 Which inventory measures are relevant?
3 Which products, locations, and time periods should be considered?
4 What factors could be contributing to the increase?
5 Which dimensions should be investigated or drilled into?
6 Which visualizations would best reveal those relationships?
7 What business rules, constraints, or context should influence the interpretation?
8 Most importantly, does the finding require any business action?

Earlier Discovery primarily helped with the data exploration and visualization part of this process. The user had to provide much of the context, determine what to investigate, select the relevant data, and decide how to interpret the result.

Earlier Discovery

“Give me the tools to explore the data.”

The opportunity for New Discovery

“Help me understand the business question, determine what to investigate, and guide me toward the relevant insight.”

05 · WHAT CHANGES

Manual and Chat Discovery serve different starting points.

The new experience does not make direct manipulation irrelevant. Instead, it adds a second way of entering the analytical experience.

Manual Discovery

“I know what I want to explore.”

The user directly controls the analytical experience.

  • • Select data and dimensions
  • • Configure filters and measures
  • • Modify visualizations
  • • Drill into details
  • • Explore known analytical questions
  • • Create repeatable analytical views
Chat Discovery

“I know the problem, but not the path.”

The user expresses intent and lets the system help discover the analytical path.

  • • Ask questions in natural language
  • • Discover relevant context automatically
  • • Generate analytical outputs
  • • Ask follow-up questions
  • • Investigate adjacent issues
  • • Move from insight toward action

What this unlocks

01

Lower entry barrier

Users can start with a business question instead of learning how to construct the analysis first.

02

Faster investigation

Less time is spent deciding which data, dimensions and visualizations to assemble.

03

Progressive discovery

The investigation can evolve naturally through follow-up questions.

04

Context-aware analysis

Relevant business context can become part of the analytical journey rather than being manually assembled.

05

Visual answers

The output is not only conversational text; analysis can be expressed visually.

06

Deeper exploration

Users can move from an initial answer into increasingly detailed investigation without starting over.

Manual Discovery gives control. Chat Discovery gives a new starting point.

The goal is not to remove manual analysis. It is to make Discovery accessible from the moment a user has a question — even when they don't yet know how that question should be analyzed.
